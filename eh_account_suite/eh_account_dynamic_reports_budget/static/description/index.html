The purpose

Make the next finance decision clearer.

Extends the by-nature, single-company Profit and Loss report with Budget, Actual-minus-Budget Variance, and Variance Percent columns. Company-wide budgets compare to company actuals. Analytic-scoped budgets compare to allocation-weighted actuals from journal-item analytic distributions. Users choose disclosed daily-overlap apportionment or exact-period validation. Budget choices show lifecycle and version identity and stay synchronized with company scope.

Inside this module

Built around the work your team does.

Selection-based activation

Budget columns appear only when you choose a budget in the report's Budget option. Without selection, the P&L displays normally. No automatic generation or module-level defaults.

Type-based section reconciliation

Income and expense section budgets are derived from account types, not summed from arbitrary budget lines. This keeps budget section totals consistent with their underlying account budgets.

Requires budget module

Depends on Multi Version Budget Pro to provide the budget records and budget line data. Cannot function standalone.

Zero budgets

Variance remains actual minus zero; percentage stays blank because no meaningful denominator exists.

Analytic scope

Analytic budgets use one plan and weighted actuals. Mixed global and analytic lines fail closed.

Budget selection in the report

Adds synchronized lifecycle, budget, and apportionment controls plus favourable/adverse variance colouring.

Supporting workflow

Compare a quarter with an annual budget

Illustrative implementation exercise. A company selects an annual budget while reviewing a quarter’s by-nature P&L. Under daily-overlap apportionment, inspect the disclosed allocation of the budget across the selected dates; under exact-period validation, use aligned periods or review the rejection of a mismatched choice. The policy decision changes the meaning of the comparison and should be visible to management. Review an account present only in the budget and check how it contributes to its section total. For an analytic-scoped budget, compare allocation-weighted actuals within the same scope. Inspect a zero-budget line without treating its percentage result as an ordinary finite growth rate. Unsupported dimensions should prompt a scope correction rather than a misleading total.

Read the four figures as one comparison

The useful question is not whether a report contains an extra column. It is whether actual and budget describe the same economic scope. Actual shows the relevant posted result; Budget shows the selected plan under the disclosed period policy; Variance is Actual minus Budget; Variance Percent expresses that difference against the budget basis. Review all four together. A positive mathematical variance does not by itself tell management whether the outcome is favourable: the interpretation also depends on whether the line represents revenue, expense or another result. When a budget is zero, a normal percentage-growth interpretation is unavailable, so begin with the absolute difference and the reason a budget was absent. Do not hide an unbudgeted cost behind a percentage label that looks routine. The module supports presentation of the comparison; the budget owner still needs to explain the commercial cause.

Choose the period policy before discussing performance

An annual plan and a quarterly actual are not automatically comparable. Daily-overlap apportionment provides a disclosed way to allocate the relevant portion of a budget period. Exact-period validation instead requires the chosen periods to align. Decide which question management is asking before choosing between them. A daily allocation can be a useful baseline, but it is not a claim that a seasonal business earns or spends evenly each day. For a seasonal plan, record suitable period budgets in the planning module and review the comparison against those intended periods. In an acceptance exercise, keep the same actual ledger and compare a matched-period case with an overlapping-period case. Explain the difference to the eventual report reader. That explanation is part of the reporting policy and should travel with a recurring management pack.

Keep analytic allocation and company totals distinct

An analytic budget needs actuals that refer to the same analytic scope. A journal item can allocate its amount across several analytic dimensions or accounts, so selecting an analytic label is not the same as assigning the full journal-item amount to it. The bridge uses allocation-weighted actuals for the supported analytic-scoped comparison. Review a known split allocation during implementation and compare it with the company-wide amount before relying on departmental totals. A budget version must have one coherent scope; mixing company-wide and analytic-scoped lines can create overlap that looks like extra spending or extra budget. The module refuses unsupported combinations instead of making the ambiguity disappear. When management asks for a different dimensional comparison, first establish a coherent budget model and check that the chosen report layout supports it.

Review accounts that have a plan but no activity

An account can have a budget without any posted actual in the selected period. Omitting that account would hide an unused plan and could distort section totals or the explanation of net profit. Include a budget-only account in the acceptance case and inspect its position inside the account hierarchy. Then add a small actual amount and compare the result. Review section totals at the currency precision shown to readers rather than relying on unrounded spreadsheet arithmetic hidden elsewhere. A changed presentation currency also needs a consistent treatment for budget and actual. The bridge’s comparison should be read as one report with one disclosed monetary basis, not a combination of independently translated columns assembled after export.

Preserve version identity in the management conversation

Two budgets can cover the same dates while representing different decisions. One might be the original approved plan and another a later working revision. Review the lifecycle and version identity presented by the budget selector before naming a report “against budget”. If users need access to a broader set of versions, make that choice explicit and keep it separate from the arithmetic. When a relevant budget line or analytic scope changes, rerun the report and inspect the refreshed result; an exported prior pack remains evidence of what was reviewed then. The bridge does not prepare, approve or forecast the budget itself. Those responsibilities belong to Budget Pro and the organisation’s planning process, while this module connects the chosen baseline to the supported P&L presentation.

Worked implementation guide

Practice the decisions behind the workflow.

Reconcile the three columns on one account

Choose one P&L account with a small, understood set of actual entries and a clearly identified budget version. Record the Budget, Variance and Variance Percent values and compare them with the stated Actual-minus-Budget convention. If the budget is analytically scoped, check the analytic allocation basis rather than comparing it with unrestricted company actuals. Repeat the example with the intended period policy: daily-overlap apportionment and exact-period validation are different choices. Keep the lifecycle and version identity with the review, especially where several budgets cover overlapping dates. A clear column explanation helps a manager understand what changed before asking why it changed.

Multi Version Budget Pro

Multi-version budgets with purchase-order encumbrance, sign-aware variance, and an optional analytic dimension, computed straight from posted journal items.

Budgets that stop a PO

An off/warn/block policy is enforced when a purchase order is confirmed, and the block path accumulates the required amount per budget line across every PO line before it checks availability. Several lines that each fit but together overrun are still caught. Commitments are idempotent per PO line, so re-confirming updates instead of duplicating.

Variance the right way round

Income, liability and equity accounts are sign-normalised before variance and availability are computed, so a revenue budget met to the dollar reads as zero variance, not a 200 percent overrun. A non-zero actual on a zero budget reads as a full 100 percent overrun rather than a silent 0 percent. The same maths runs in the ORM compute and the SQL report view, parity-tested.

Yours to keep

LGPL-3 source on disk. No activation key, no phone-home, no recurring licence. Multi company aware, posted-only actuals computed in one batch SQL per budget so it stays fast on hundreds of lines. Read it, extend it, fork it if you need to.

COMMITMENTS

Two purchase-order lines that resolve to the same budget line keep separate commitments keyed by PO line, instead of one silently overwriting the other. Re-confirming a PO updates the existing commitments rather than creating duplicates.

OVERRUN

The block policy accumulates the required amount per budget line across multiple PO lines before checking availability, so several lines that each individually fit but collectively overrun the budget are still caught at confirm time.

VARIANCE

A non-zero actual against a zero budget returns 100 percent to flag a full overrun, instead of being reported as a misleading 0 percent.

SIGN

Actuals on credit-natured accounts (income, liability, equity) are sign-flipped before variance and availability are computed, so a 10k revenue budget against 10k actual reads as zero variance, not a 200 percent overrun. The report view replicates the same normalisation.

RELEASE

A billed purchase order with a zero or negative untaxed total releases its commitment fully rather than dividing by zero when computing the proportional release.
